## 🔆 Abstract

This study examines the environmental impacts of aluminum production for electric vehicle (EV) applications, focusing on key processes such as **bauxite mining**, **alumina refining**, **aluminum smelting**, and **ingot casting**.

A **cradle-to-gate Life Cycle Assessment (LCA)** was conducted using **SimaPro 9.3.0.2** with the following impact assessment methods:

- CML IA Baseline  
- TRACI 2.1  
- ReCiPe 2016 Endpoint  

The study quantified environmental impacts in critical categories including **Global Warming Potential (GWP)**, resource depletion, and toxicity.

### Key Findings

- **Aluminum ingot production** is the most environmentally intensive stage, generating **13.5 kg CO₂ eq per kg of aluminum**, mainly due to electricity consumption (**5.86 kg CO₂ eq**).
- **Smelting** contributed **3.96 kg CO₂ eq/kg**, largely driven by fossil fuel-based energy.
- **Bauxite mining** showed the lowest Global Warming Potential (**0.0672 kg CO₂ eq**).
- **Alumina refining** produced significant emissions (**0.821 kg CO₂ eq**) primarily from natural gas and fuel oil usage.

Greenhouse gas emissions, acidification, and high energy consumption were identified as the major contributors to environmental damage.

### Recommendations

The results highlight the urgent need to:
- Transition to **renewable energy** sources
- Improve **energy efficiency** in smelting and refining
- Increase the use of **recycled aluminum**

This study provides actionable insights for achieving more sustainable aluminum production in the electric vehicle industry and supports global decarbonization efforts.
